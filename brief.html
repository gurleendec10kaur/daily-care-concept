    Two user stories with acceptance criteria

    As a member who checks glucose daily, I want the app to warn me before I run out of strips and let me reorder in one tap, so that I never miss a reading. Now
    daily usestrips a day×lead time + 3delivery days + buffer=reorder pointe.g. 2 × 7 = 14
    1. Count: each synced reading lowers the strip count by one; the member can correct it.
    2. Warn: when strips cover 7 days or fewer (14-day average use), a banner shows strips left and the run-out date.
    3. Order: tap Reorder and confirm → order placed, banner shows "on the way", tracking in the app.
    4. Automatic: "when I'm running low" orders at the reorder point; fixed 30/60/90 days ship lead-time days early. The member can order earlier anytime.
    5. Speed: if standard would arrive after run-out, upgrade to express at no cost to the member; they are not asked to choose.
    6. Remind: one notification 3 days before each automatic order: ship date, quantity, "covered by your plan" or amount and card; confirm, skip or change from it.
    7. No card if covered: plan-covered members never see a payment method.
    8. Control: skip, pause or turn off in two taps or fewer, no phone call.
    9. Restock: on delivery, the count increases by the quantity delivered.
    Why usage-based, not a fixed subscription

    Testing frequency changes with illness, medicines and doctor advice, so a fixed date over- or under-supplies, and the app already knows how many strips are used. Weekly boxes also cost more to ship. The trigger is a reorder point (demand during delivery lead time plus safety stock), the same logic used in supply chain planning, applied to one member. Open questions: actual delivery times by region, the cost of free express, plan limits on strips per month, and how fulfilment and billing work today.

    As a member in both the blood pressure and Mind programs, I want to see whether my stress relates to my BP readings, so that I understand what affects my numbers and what I can do about it. Next
    1. Enough data: shown only with 10+ of the last 14 days having both a BP reading and a stress check-in.
    2. Compare: average systolic BP on high-stress days (4–5) vs other days; shown only above a threshold set with the clinical team.
    3. Safe wording: "a pattern in your own data, not a diagnosis"; no medication advice; copy approved by the clinical team.
    4. Act: start a 2-minute exercise, share with the coach, or hide insights of this type.
    5. Support: high stress on 5+ of the last 14 days shows coaching, care options and crisis resources.

    How I'd measure it

    North starEngaged members per month as a % of enrolled members
    The unit Dario is paid on

    Now

    Strip run-outs per 100 glucose members a month
    % of boxes arriving before run-out
    % on automatic delivery; % of reorders in-app
    Support tickets per 1,000 members; median first reply
    Check-in answer rate; % active again in 7 days, by reason
    30- and 90-day retention of new members

    Next

    % of eligible members who view an insight and do the exercise
    % of members active in 2+ programs
    Daily plan completion
    % who add a caregiver; their 90-day retention vs others

    Guardrails

    Overstock: boxes shipped with 14+ days of strips left
    Automatic delivery skip and turn-off rate
    Billing complaints per 1,000 members
    Notification opt-outs; crash-free sessions
    "Hide insight" rate
    How to test: roll out Now items to one client cohort first; A/B test the insight card vs no card for eligible members over 8 weeks, measuring 30-day engagement.
